Nudgee Catholic Cemetery, Brisbane

Nudgee

Nineteen of this family are buried here with a plot number and an exact date — and four of those plots are consecutive.

Nudgee Cemetery, beside Holy Cross church at Banyo, has been the Catholic burying ground of northern Brisbane since the 1860s. It is run by the Archdiocese, not the city council, which is why it does not appear in the Brisbane City Council grave search and why the family tree recorded the cemetery but never a plot.

The Archdiocese keeps its own register, and it was read on 10 September 2026. It supplies a plot, a register number, a date of birth, a date of death and a date of interment for 19 people in this archive — and it corrects the family tree in 6 places.

The finding

Four plots in a row

Michael Rocco Mazza's four grandparents were born in three towns — Piedimonte Etneo on Etna, Briatico on the Tyrrhenian coast, and Scilla on the strait — about six hundred kilometres apart, in families that had never met. They arrived in Australia between 1949 and 1953 on four different sailings. They are buried in four consecutive plots.

Where the register beats the tree

6 corrections

A burial register is a contemporary administrative record made by the people who did the burying. A family tree is a recollection typed in later. Where they disagree, this archive believes the register — and shows you both, so you can disagree with it.

One of these is not a mistake but a format. The tree gives Venera Cavallaro 's death as 9 June 2013; the register says 6 September 2013. 9/6 and 6/9 — a date written day-first and read month-first, or the reverse. It is the commonest single error in transcribed genealogy and it is worth checking every time a date in this archive looks a month or two out.

The Falco lie here too

This archive keeps meeting the same ground from a different direction: five Falco of Arienzo are buried at Nudgee, and that archive found them independently, in the same register, using the same endpoint. Two families with nothing whatever in common in Italy — one from Campania, three from Calabria and Sicily — inside the same fence in Queensland. That is not a family connection. It is what a migration looks like from the outside.

Arneytown, New Jersey: one soldier, one grave

The tree records three Polistena at the Brigadier General William C. Doyle Veterans Memorial Cemetery. The VA's Nationwide Gravesite Locator records two people, in one plot:

O3, 8647
POLISTENA, JOSEPH — PFC US ARMY, World War II
29 January 1917 – 15 February 2012
O3, 8647
POLISTENA, SANTA
17 December 1920 – 8 September 2008

Joseph Polistena served as a Private First Class in the United States Army in the Second World War — that is the service that earned the plot. Santa is entered as wife of, in the same site.

It also settles two things this archive had open. The tree's "Joseph A Polistena" and "Joseph Polistena" are one man — which is exactly what the build's middle-initial merge rule concluded from the shared death date, before the VA confirmed it. And Santa's death, given in the tree as 9 August 2008, is 8 September 2008: the American 09/08/2008 read day-first. That is the second date-format error found in this archive today, after Venera Cavallaro 's.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
