The Marriages

Who married whom, and which families came with them. Every surname in this archive is here for one reason — that a person married a person — and this page says which person, for each.

Why this page exists

This archive used to list its 124 surnames with a count against each and claim nothing about any of them. That was honest, and it was also silent: it could not say why those names were here. They are here because a person married a person, and the person graph has known which person all along.

A family is placed by descent, never by spelling. Born a Mazza or descended from one; married such a person; the family that person came from; or married into one of those. 14 carry this family's blood, 17 married one, 36 are such a family, 49 married into one of those, and 8 cannot yet be reached from it at all. Nothing below matches a name against a name: every link walked is a parent, child or spouse edge carried in the data.

The living are named and nothing more. 411 people are held as living: a name and a relationship, no date and no place. Where a family shows no named people at all, every person the archive holds for that surname is living.

What would help

  • A marriage act. It names all four parents at once, which is the only record that can turn a «married one» into a «came from».
  • A surname recorded once. Most of the thin entries above are one person who married in and left no further trace. They are kept rather than dropped, because dropping them would quietly make this family look more endogamous than it was.
  • Anything from the Sicilian and Calabrian side after 1900 — the registers close to the public at a century; the family's own papers do not.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski

  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
