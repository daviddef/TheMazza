Catania, Sicily

Riposto

The lower town, separate from Giarre since 1821 — and the only comune left that fits the man this archive cannot find.

21–22 September 2026 · where the search stands

Why this town, and not another

Giovanni Nicotra is the last unfound person on the Sicilian spine. His own marriage certificate says he was nato a Giarre; eleven Giarre birth years, read end to end in both the main run and the appendix, do not hold him. Mascali, where he was living in 1872, does not hold him either — nine years read there.

Riposto is the lower town, the port below Giarre, and it has registered separately since 1821 — through the whole window in which he was born. A man of the lower town could say Giarre and be entered here. That is a reading from general history and not from any document, and it is written down as such.

What makes it more than a guess is what the registers hold. Giarre's only Nicotra across eleven years is a Dottor Don with a Donna wife. Mascali's across nine is an Alfio. Riposto has three ordinary Nicotra households — the sort of family a bracciante belongs to.

And in September 2026 the reading stopped being an inference. Riposto's marriage Tavola for 1844 records Nicotra Salvadore, patria Giarre, marrying a Riposto woman — and his two children then appear in Riposto's own birth Tavole, in 1845 and 1847. A Nicotra who says Giarre and is registered here is no longer a theory about how people moved; it is a man with an act number.

None of them is Rosario. Giovanni Nicotra's father is named in the 1872 act as Rosario, a bracciante of Mascali, with Venera Murabito — and no household of that name has been found in any of the three towns. Four Riposto years remain unread.

1841 was the one unfinished negative, and it is finished now. The first volume's alphabet runs M into P with its last leaf torn and the right page lost — and Giarre had already taught this archive that an appendix can sit past the end of the alphabet. There is a second 1841 volume (17517, suppl. 2), and it carries its own untorn Tavola: M runs into O with no N heading at all. The O it holds is exactly what the torn leaf lost. Two volumes for one year is not two witnesses — a duplicate is a copy — but the copy reaches the part the original cannot show.

1844 is the one that pays. Atto 39 marries Nicotra Salvadore to Maria Barbagallo of Riposto — and the patria column gives his as GIARRE, with his parents named as Alfio Nicotra and Rosa Pappalardo. He is the father of the two Riposto children the birth sweep had already turned up, in 1845 and 1847. Here is a Nicotra born at Giarre, married at Riposto, and registering his children at Riposto — which is precisely the shape this archive had guessed at for Giovanni, and had no document for until now.

The restauration run stops at 1860 and the Italian one begins at 1866 — a five-year hole in the middle, 1861 to 1865, which is the unification gap this archive has met at other comuni. It matters here only if the answer turns out to lie in it.

What would end this

Twenty-three Riposto years are read and Rosario is in none of them. That is now a finding rather than a gap.

  • The Giarre marriages. Salvadore Nicotra was born at Giarre and married at Riposto. If Rosario is his brother — and nothing yet says he is — then Rosario's own marriage to Venera Murabito is most likely to sit in Giarre's or Mascali's marriage Tavole, neither of which has been swept.
  • Salvadore Nicotra's own birth act at Giarre. He married in 1844, so he was born about 1815–1824 — before every Giarre year this archive has read. That act would name Alfio Nicotra's age and trade, and would say whether this is a bracciante family or the Dottor Don one.
  • The deaths. Rosario and Venera were alive in October 1872 and no death has been sought for either, in any of the four towns.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
