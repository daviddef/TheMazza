The Sicilian Registers

Nine annual indexes and six acts, hunting one man. He is still missing — and in looking for him the archive found Rosario, and a generation above him nobody knew was there.

The family tree says Salvatore Mazza was born at Piedimonte Etneo in 1879, son of a Rosario Mazza about whom exactly one thing was known: that he existed. Finding Salvatore's act of birth was supposed to name Rosario and settle everything.

Salvatore is not in the register — not in 1875, 1876, 1877, 1878, 1879, 1880, 1881 or 1882 at Piedimonte Etneo, nor at Mascali or Giarre in 1879. But the five Mazza births that are there, and two marriage acts, have between them built three documented households, proved two of the men brothers, and reached a generation the family tree does not contain at all.

The finding

Innocenzio Mazza and Domenica Cavallaro

A birth act names a father. A marriage act names both fathers and both mothers — which is why two marriages were worth more than five births. Two of them, two years apart, name the same couple:

1874, atto 26
Antonino Mazza, di anni trentuno, bracciante, qui nato, figlio d'Innocenzio, bracciante, e di Domenica Cavallaro, industriosa — marries Rosa Raiti, 20, daughter of Giuseppe Raiti and Agata Pennisi.
19 July 1874
1876, atto 5
Mazza Salvatore, di anni trentaquattro, bracciante, nato in Piedimonte Etneo, figlio di Senzio e di Cavallaro Domenica — marries Concetta Sciacca, 19, daughter of the late Antonino Sciacca and Rosaria Greco.
16 January 1876

Salvatore and Antonino are brothers — born about 1841 and 1843, both braccianti of Piedimonte Etneo, both sons of Domenica Cavallaro. Two independent acts, two years apart, naming the same mother. Innocenzio Mazza and Domenica Cavallaro are the earliest Mazza this archive can name from a document — a couple of the 1810s or 1820s — and nothing in Michael Mazza's family tree reaches them.

A correction, in the open

This page said «Venerio» for six hours, and it was wrong

Reading act 5 alone, this archive rendered the father's name as Venerio — and then built an argument on it: that Salvatore's son of 1879, christened Venerio, was named for his paternal grandfather in the ordinary Sicilian way, so custom and document agreed. It was a satisfying piece of reasoning and it was built on a misreading.

Act 26 of 1874 spells the same man's name out in full: Innocenzio. Magnified, act 5 says «Senzio» — the standard Sicilian contraction of Innocenzio, not Venerio at all.

So the naming inference is withdrawn. Venerio Mazza, born 1879, is not named for his paternal grandfather, and where the name came from is now an open question — the Cavallaro side, a godparent, or a great-grandfather nobody has reached yet.

The lesson is the one this archive keeps relearning: a single document read once is a hypothesis. It took a second act, and a magnified crop, to catch it — and the elegant inference is exactly the kind that stops you looking.

Rosario is real, and he is here. Aged 31 in October 1877 and 32 in May 1880 — born about 1846–48 — a bracciante, married to Leonarda Bonaccorso, with sons Orazio and Gaetano. The archive has carried him as a name with a single asserted fact attached. He now has an age, a trade, a wife, two children and three streets.

Why this matters for the hundred and ten

The join, and what still holds it open

The second component of the family tree — a hundred and ten Piedimonte Etneo Mazza joined to this family by nothing — is headed by a Rosario Mazza born 1850, and among his children the tree lists an Orazio.

The register gives a Rosario Mazza born about 1846–48, of the same small town, whose son is Orazio. That is a far better correspondence than a shared surname — the right man, the right decade, the right town, and the right child's name. It is still not proof. The tree dates Orazio to 1883; the register to 1877. Two Rosario Mazza with two sons called Orazio in one town is unlikely, but this archive has already been burned once by assuming that unlikely means impossible.

What would close it: Rosario Mazza's own marriage act. It would give his parents — and if they are Venerio Mazza and Domenica Cavallaro, then Rosario and Salvatore are brothers, the whole Via Terremorte kindred is one family, and the tree's hundred and ten hang off a documented couple. It is not in the 1875 or 1876 marriage registers, both of which have been read, so it is 1874 or earlier.

The acts

Two corrections this made

The index is not the act. The 1877 annual index lists act 168 as Mazza Grazia. The act itself says Orazio — a boy, not a girl. An index is a clerk's abstract and it can be wrong; the act is the record.

And a misreading of my own, corrected by a second document. Reading act 205 alone, this archive gave Salvatore Mazza's wife as Greco Concetta — which produced an absurdity, two women of that name on one street bearing children six weeks apart. The 1876 marriage index settles it: Salvatore married Concetta Sciacca. It is Pasquale whose wife is Concetta Greco, and that is confirmed twice, in 1877 and 1879. Two documents beat one, and the absurdity was the tell.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
