221 things the records say differently

For Michael

Every place where a document and the family tree disagree, or where a document says something the tree does not say at all — with the source for each, so every line can be checked.

This page is addressed to the person who built the tree. An archive that only ever agrees with its source is not doing any work — so here is the list of places where this one disagrees, in the open, with the citation attached to every line.

Nothing here has been applied. The charts on this site draw Michael's export exactly as it was given, because it is his research and the corrections are proposals, not edits. 11 of the 221 are marked candidate or flagged rather than documented, which means the archive thinks it is probably right and is telling you it is not certain.

The commonest single error is not a wrong name. It is a wrong year — and almost always a birth year, on a person the tree could not date from a document. Giovanni Arena is out by eleven years, Michele Donato by five, Salvatore Mazza by six. That pattern is worth knowing when you look at any other undated birth in the tree.

Rocco Arena's brothers and sister, and a cousin Missing people — TWO NEW GENERATIONS

was Not in the tree

now FILIPPO ARENA b. 14 February 1852, DOMENICO ARENA b. 24 March 1853 and GIUSEPPE ARENA b. 5 March 1860 are all sons of Francesco Antonio Arena and Serafina Arlotta -- Rocco's elder brothers. The eldest is named FILIPPO for his grandfather, exactly as Calabrian custom predicts, which is a third independent support for the Filippo Arena generation. Separately, TERESA ARENA b. 17 October 1853 is a daughter of Orazio Arena and Giovanna Zirilli -- Orazio's earliest known child, and the tree gives him no daughters at all. AMENDED 20 SEPTEMBER: «DOMENICO ARENA b. 24 March 1853» IS DOMENICA ARENA, A DAUGHTER. Act 53 of 1853 -- not act 2, which is a Tavola row number -- says «una FEMINA», «di dare alla FANCIULLA il nome di DOMENICA», and names DOMENICA ARENA in the baptism column. SO ROCCO HAD TWO BROTHERS DOCUMENTED HERE, not three: Filippo (1852) and Giuseppe (1860), besides Giovanni (1854). The row is left standing with this note rather than rewritten, because the mistake is part of the record.

Scilla, Nati 1852 atto 48; Nati 1853 atti 2 and 6; Nati 1860 atto 45

Orazio Arena Generation — a lead, RETRACTED

was Nothing above him

now THIS ARCHIVE WITHDRAWS ITS OWN INFERENCE. From Orazio naming two sons Domenico, naming custom suggested his father was a Domenico Arena. Two Orazio Arena births at Scilla now say otherwise: act 10 of 1821 gives him a father GIUSEPPE (and the tree's own year, 1821, exactly), and act 1 of 1833 gives a different Orazio a father BONAVENTURA. Neither is a Domenico. The mistake was not the custom but the assumption behind it: the first child this archive had FOUND was not necessarily his FIRST child. The years 1850-1855 are unread, and a son Giuseppe in one of them would restore the custom exactly. Recorded as a candidate: ORAZIO ARENA b. 14 DECEMBER 1821, son of Giuseppe Arena and a Teresa. FURTHER EVIDENCE, BOTH WAYS: Orazio's earliest known child is a DAUGHTER, Teresa, born 17 October 1853. Custom names the first daughter for her paternal grandmother, and act 10 of 1821 makes Orazio's mother a Teresa -- which SUPPORTS the 1821 candidate. But that same act makes his father a Giuseppe, and Orazio's first son is Domenico, with 1852-1855 now read and no earlier son. The two halves disagree and the archive is not picking one.

Scilla, Nati 1821 atto 10; Nati 1833 atto 1

How to read the confidence

Six labels, and the counts are counted rather than typed — two of these were in use here for days with no entry on this list, which is its own small lesson about legends written by hand.

documented
A record has been read and it says this. You can open the citation and see it.
176
certain
Established beyond doubt, but by setting two documents against each other rather than by reading one — an original against a chancery copy, a declared age against a birth act. Several of the entries above are corrections this archive made to a nineteenth-century copyist, and one is a correction it made to itself.
21
probable
Either the best reading of an ambiguous document, or two documents that disagree — with the alternative stated in the row rather than hidden. Where a clerk crammed two ages into one clause, where a surname reached an index garbled, or where a register and a chancery copy give a woman two different names, this is what the answer is worth.
7
inferred
No record names this person directly, but the step from what a record does say is a short one — someone travelling on the same day and the same ship, or a birthplace that only one of two candidate households can account for. An inference from circumstance, not from a hard-to-read hand.
6
candidate
The best match in an index, with something still not quite right about it — a spelling, an age. Probably correct. Not proved.
4
flagged
A problem identified and deliberately not resolved, because resolving it would need a record nobody has read yet.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.

An evidence-first archive of the Mazza family of Piedimonte Etneo in Sicily — and of Briatico and Scilla in Calabria, of New York and Buenos Aires, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence, or is marked as unproven. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
