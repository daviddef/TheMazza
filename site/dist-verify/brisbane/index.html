The first one out

Michele Mazza was born at Catania in 1909 and landed at Fremantle on 21 April 1925, aged about sixteen. He died in Queensland the following year. He is the earliest Australian arrival in this archive and the shortest life in it, and he came out a full twenty-four years before the family that followed him.

Whether he was the reason the others chose Queensland — whether there was already a Mazza name at an address in Brisbane when Sebastiano Mazza stepped off at Fremantle in 1949 — is not something the archive can presently answer. It is a good question and it is written down. Open questions →

A family that arrived sideways

The Crupi, who came through a marriage

The family tree has one of them, and it has her name wrong. “Angela Cupo” is ANGELA CRUPI, born 20 December 1900, who married Rosario Mazza and lies at Nudgee. Her Queensland death registration of 1973 names her parents — Francesco Crupi and Maria Vecchio — and the same two names appear on three more registrations.

She had brothers here, and nobody knew. Pietro, Antonino and Mario Crupi all died in Queensland and all three registrations give the same parents as hers. Two of them — Pietro and Antonino — came into Brisbane together on the OTRANTO on 19 August 1930, consecutive entries in the Brisbane aliens register. Angela followed on the VIMINALE on 7 December 1937 with two daughters, aged fifteen and eight — an arrival this archive had no record of at all.

Antonino was interned. His aliens card is stamped LOVEDAY 35924; the reverse records him moved “ex Gaythorne to 14A” on 16 March 1943 and released back to Queensland on 22 October 1943. He was a boot repairer at Ingham, five feet four, black eyes, black hair, the fourth finger of his right hand deformed — and born at Piedimonte Etneo, the Mazza's own town.

And one absence turned out to have a reason. Pietro has no aliens registration card, which looked like a gap. NAA: BP4/3, ITALIAN CRUPI P records “Alien Registration Certificate NOT ISSUED”, the folio noting that he was naturalised on 23 February 1932. By 1939 he was a British subject and outside the series. An explained absence and an unexplained one look identical until somebody checks.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch

An evidence-first archive of the Mazza family of Piedimonte Etneo in Sicily — and of Briatico and Scilla in Calabria, of New York and Buenos Aires, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence, or is marked as unproven. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
