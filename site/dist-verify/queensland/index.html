The Queensland Register

The Registry's historical index is free, it is authoritative, and — the part that matters — it can be searched by a father's or a mother's name. One search returned three of Sebastiano Mazza's siblings.

The Queensland Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages publishes a searchable historical index: deaths to 1996, marriages to 1951, births to 1926. Every death and birth entry carries the names of both parents, and the search box will take a parent's name as the query.

That turns it from a lookup into a reconstruction tool. Searching "Salvatore Mazza" in the father field returns the deaths of his children — and each of those names their mother, which is how a family asserted by the tree became a family on the record.

The finding

Michele Mazza was Sebastiano's brother

Michele Mazza landed at Brisbane on the Palermo on 21 April 1925, aged about sixteen, and died the following year. The family tree asserted he was kin and produced nothing to show it. The archive has carried the question since the day it was built: was he the reason they chose Queensland?

The Registry's index answers it:

Michali Mazza · death registration 14 November 1926 · 1926/C/3983 · father Salvatori Mazza · mother Maria Lecota.

Lecota is the index's rendering of Nicotra — and the same search returns Rosario Mazza, born 1899, whose parents are given in full as Salvatore Mazza and Maria Concetta Nicotra. The same couple, spelled properly.

So Michele Mazza was a son of Salvatore Mazza and Maria Nicotra, and therefore Sebastiano Mazza 's elder brother. He came out first, alone, at sixteen. He was dead within nineteen months. Twenty-four years later his family followed him to the port he had landed at.

The archive should say what it still cannot: whether anyone was waiting for Sebastiano in 1949, and whether the family knew where the boy was buried. The index gives a date and a registration number, not a grave. C in the registration means a country event — he did not die in Brisbane.

Michali Mazza — Lecota is the index's garbling of Nicotra. Makes Michele a SON of Salvatore Mazza and Maria Nicotra, and therefore Sebastiano's brother. C = country event, outside Brisbane.

Rosario Mazza (b. 1899) — Gives Maria Nicotra's full name. Confirms Rosario as another son. Matches the Nudgee interment of 22 March 1986, plot V-0117. His wife is now named: ANGELA MAZZA, born CRUPI, in the adjoining plot V-0116, dead 10 January 1973.

Maria Nicotra Mazza — Confirms the family tree's parentage for her exactly. Matches the Nudgee interment of 25 September 1963, plot V-0044.

Mary Rosa Mazza — Confirms Sebastiano Mazza and Domenica Prostamo as her parents. Matches the Nudgee interment of 10 August 1968, plot V-0165. She was sixteen.

Helen Mazza — THE TROVE FUNERAL NOTICE, NAMED AT LAST. The Brisbane Courier of Tuesday 26 June 1928, page 12, carried "Mazza.—The Funeral … Lut-wyche-road, Wooloowin … to the Nudgee R.C. Cemetery" — a second Mazza death this archive did not hold. This is it: a death on the 24th, a funeral on the 26th. Her own parents are Scottish, so Mazza is her married name. WHICH Mazza she married is not shown, and she is not in the Nudgee rows this archive holds. A candidate, not a relative.

Mario Mazza — Probably the groom of 1924 — his father a Rosario, his son a Rosario, which is the same naming rule this family uses and the reason the two households are easy to confuse.

James Lawrence Mazza — Son of the 1944 marriage below. An anglicised Mazza household, unconnected here.

Giovanni Mazza — A third Queensland Mazza household. No link shown.

Guiseppi Mazza — A fourth. No link shown.

Andrea Mazza — The index gives only the surname for the father and a dash for the mother. Carried because the surname list is now complete and this is one of its seventeen.

Josephine Mazza — Born a MAZZAGLIA and died a MAZZA. The Mazzaglia surname already runs through this index beside the Mazza — Mario Mazza married Peppina Mazzaglia in 1924 — so the two families married more than once.

Angela Mazza — THE ANGELA BURIED BESIDE ROSARIO, IDENTIFIED — AND SHE IS THE TREE'S «ANGELA CUPO». The export carries Rosario Mazza's wife as ANGELA CUPO, born 1900 at Catania and DIED 1973 AT BRISBANE, with no parents. The Nudgee register interred an Angela Mazza on 13 JANUARY 1973 in plot V-0116, beside Rosario in V-0117; this registration puts her death on 10 JANUARY 1973 and names her parents FRANCESCO CRUPI and MARIA VECCHIO. Three days, one woman, and CUPO IS THIS DATA'S GARBLING OF CRUPI — the same hand that wrote «Lecota» for Nicotra and «Postamo» for Prostamo. A CORRECTION OF MY OWN: this row first pointed at «angela-mazza», who is a different and LIVING woman — Frank Mazza and Nancy Arena's daughter, married to Reinhard Muller. The identification was right; the person it was hung on was not.

Maria Assunta Prostamo (b. 1936) — THE FIRST PROSTAMO EVER FOUND IN THE QUEENSLAND INDEX. Born 1936, died 27 October 1991. She was born a MELLUSO and died a PROSTAMO — a Melluso married a Prostamo a second time, two generations after Alfonso Prostamo married Caterina Melluso at Briatico on 21 May 1887. Trove independently has her: «Prostamo, Assunta Maria, 3 Frank-street, Graceville», naturalised 4 July 1966 and gazetted that November. Two sources, one person. How she joins Alfonso's line is UNTESTED — a Melluso marrying a Prostamo in Brisbane is a strong hint, not a descent.

Gregorio Melluso — Maria Assunta's father, and with him two more names: GENNARO MELLUSO and MARIA LIMARDO. Limardo is a Potenzoni surname — it appears in the Potenzoni birth index of 1818 — which places this Melluso household in the Briatico-Potenzoni district rather than the Agrigento Melluso that Geneanet is full of. C = a country registration, outside Brisbane.

Mario Mazza (b. 1900) — RESOLVES A HOUSEHOLD THIS ARCHIVE CARRIED AS UNJOINED. The index already held a Mario Mazza marrying Peppina Mazzaglia on 29 October 1924 and their son Rosario born the next year, noted here as «not this family so far as anything shows». This is almost certainly the same Mario — born 1900, died 1986 — and it names his parents at last: ROSARIO MAZZA and ROSA GRASSO. Still not joined to Salvatore Mazza's household; but it is now a named family rather than a loose pair.

Giovanni Mazza (b. 1958) — A Brisbane Mazza household new to this archive: ANGELO MAZZA and FRANCESCA STRANO. No join shown.

Guiseppi Mazza (b. 1932) — Another household new to this archive: STEFANO MAZZA and GRAZIA GUARERRA. No join shown.

Pietro Crupi — ANGELA CRUPI'S BROTHER. Same two parents as her own registration of 1973 -- FRANCESCO CRUPI and MARIA VECCHIO -- and this one gives the father as «Francesco» without a surname, which is how the index often renders him. Not in the family tree and not in this archive until today.

Antonino Crupi — A SECOND BROTHER. «Antonino Crupi», the same parents again. A Marriage of an ANTONINO CRUPI is registered on 24 August 1940 (1940/C/3314) and is very likely his.

Mario Crupi — A THIRD BROTHER, and the last of the four. Same parents. A MARIANO CRUPI marries on 31 January 1940 (1940/C/349) -- Mario and Mariano are the same name in these records, so that is probably his.

Where it points next

Rosario, born 1899, dates his father

This archive still cannot find Salvatore Mazza's birth — six years of the Piedimonte Etneo register, plus Mascali and Giarre, and he is in none of them.

The Queensland index has just handed over the way in. Rosario Mazza was born in 1899 to Salvatore and Maria Concetta Nicotra. An Italian birth act states the declaring father's age. Find Rosario's act of birth — Piedimonte Etneo, 1899 — and it dates Salvatore to within a year or two, which turns a blind sweep of the register into a single lookup. The queue →

Method

familyhistory.bdm.qld.gov.au — free to search, no account needed; images are paid and were not bought. The form's useful fields are Father's/parent's name(s) and Mother's/parent's name(s), which work on births and deaths only. Search the parent, not the person, when you are trying to find siblings. Spellings in the index are as the clerk wrote them — Michali, Salvatori, Lecota, Postamo — so search loosely and expect the surname you want to be mangled.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
