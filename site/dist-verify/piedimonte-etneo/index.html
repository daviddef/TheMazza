Piedimonte Etneo

The town on the north-east flank of Etna that gave this family its surname — and, of the four quarters, the one the archive knows least about.

The town

Piedimonte Etneo means the foot of the mountain, and it is exactly that: a comune of the province of Catania standing on the north-eastern slope of Etna, above Fiumefreddo and the Alcantara valley, looking out at the Ionian Sea. The neighbouring towns in this archive — Mascali, Giarre, Linguaglossa's side of the mountain — are all within a few kilometres.

Living on Etna is a recurring administrative fact, not a metaphor. In 1928 the eruption that destroyed the town of Mascali — the only town Etna has swallowed whole in modern times — happened eight years after Sebastiano Mazza was born here, and the archive records Mascali as a birthplace for people in this family.

The Sicilian quarter is four towns, not one

Piedimonte Etneo gave the family its surname. It did not give it everybody.

For most of this archive's life the Sicilian side was a single town, and the family tree still says so: it puts Maria Nicotra at Piedimonte Etneo, where her husband was born. Four consecutive documents, read in September 2026, take her and her father somewhere else — and the towns are all within eight kilometres of each other, down the same coast road.

  • Piedimonte Etneo — the Mazza, back to Mariano Mazza and Maria Catalano, both alive and working there in April 1871. Also where Venera Cassaniti was born in 1848, and where she and Giovanni Nicotra married on 7 October 1872.
  • Mascali — where Giovanni Nicotra was living in 1872, and where Maria Nicotra was born on 8 December 1875, at half past nine in the evening, in contrada Sopra Pietrafitta. Her birth act was found here after fourteen consecutive Piedimonte birth indexes had been read without her, because a wife went to her husband's comune and the children were registered there.
  • Giarre — where the 1872 publication certificate says Giovanni Nicotra himself was nato. Eleven Giarre birth years have now been read end to end, main runs and appendices, and they do not hold him; the only Nicotra there in that whole decade is a Dottor Don with a Donna wife, and Giovanni's father was a bracciante.
  • Riposto — the lower town, which has registered separately from Giarre since 1821. It holds the ordinary Nicotra households that Giarre does not: two of them in 1847 alone. He is not found yet, and this is where the search now is.

None of that was in the family's export, and none of it changes who Michael Rocco Mazza's grandparents were. It changes where his great-grandmother was born, and it adds three comuni and about two thousand volumes to what this archive has to read. The Nicotra line → · What has been read →

Four generations. It is the shortest of the four quarters by five generations, and the surname the whole archive is named after runs out at a man about whom precisely one fact is known: that he was Salvatore's father.

The hundred and eleven

The family tree also contains 7 Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo — Mariano, Leonardo, Orazio, Umberto, Giuseppa, Serena, Graziella and the rest — who form a complete little world of their own: parents, children, marriages, cousins. Not one record joins them to Sebastiano's line.

They are almost certainly the same family. A town of a few thousand people does not produce two unrelated Mazza kindreds. But "almost certainly" is not a document, and the join has to be made by a record rather than by an assumption — most likely by the birth act of Salvatore Mazza, 1879, which would name his father Rosario and his father's age, or by Rosario's own marriage act. Until then this archive keeps them apart and says why. The open questions →

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch

Every claim carries its confidence, or is marked as unproven. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
