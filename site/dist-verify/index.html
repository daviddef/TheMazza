This is an archive of the Mazza of Brisbane — and of the three southern Italian towns they came out of, none of which had heard of the other two. It is not a pedigree: of the 592 people recorded here, all but a handful never continued the line. Only 68 of them have a death recorded anywhere — which is a measure of how much of this tree is still undated rather than of who is still alive.

This archive opened with the Mazza name reaching back four generations and stopping at a man with no dates at all. One day in the Sicilian registers took it to seven generations and Mariano Mazza and Maria Catalano, and joined a hundred and ten cousins to the line — every step a document.

The same day cost the archive its other headline. The Calabrian Prostamo were said to run nine generations to 1697. Audited, they are documented to a sailor dead before 1886, and asserted for four generations above him.

It is a public archive with one rule: a living person is named and nothing more — and dates are withheld for anyone eighty or younger, living or not. And a second rule, which is harder: every claim says how well it is known. Documented Inferred Family lore

Where this family can actually be put

Calabrian towns on one side of the strait, four Sicilian comuni within ten kilometres on the other, and a Brisbane parish where the quarters met. Every dot is a place this archive can put a named person in, and its size is how many stand there. The biggest are labelled; the colours are this archive's own regions. The coast and the borders under them are real — Natural Earth, clipped to this frame — so a dot can be read as somewhere rather than as a position in an empty box.

Where they came from

Four grandparents, three towns, and about six hundred kilometres of coast between the furthest two. Nothing in the Italian record joins these families to each other. They were joined in Queensland.

Briatico
Vibo Valentia, Calabria · 117 events · from 1697

Prostamo, Melluso, Anile, Barbuto, Ventrice. A comune on the Tyrrhenian coast that sent people to New York, to Buenos Aires and to Brisbane in three separate departures.
Scilla
Reggio Calabria · 78 events · from 1821

Arena, Polistena, Donato. The rock and the fishing quarter at the northern gate of the Strait of Messina — the whirlpool of the Odyssey.
Piedimonte Etneo
Catania, Sicily · 41 events · from 1879

Mazza, Nicotra. Literally the foot of the mountain: a town on the north-east flank of Etna, and the origin of the surname this archive is named for.
The Nicotra
the maternal Sicilian line · four comuni

Three days ago this line was two names on a Queensland death certificate. It now reaches generation six on both sides — and the man it is named for is still missing.
Mascali
Catania, Sicily · opened 21 Sep 2026 · from 1820

Where Maria Nicotra was born on 8 December 1875, at half past nine in the evening — found after fourteen years had been read in the wrong town. Etna swallowed the place whole in 1928.
Giarre
Catania, Sicily · opened 21 Sep 2026 · 808 volumes

The town a document names and the registers contradict. Eleven birth years read end to end, and the only Nicotra in them is a Dottor Don.
Riposto
Catania, Sicily · opened 21 Sep 2026 · separate since 1821

The lower town, fifteen hundred metres from Giarre and registering separately for seventy years. Three ordinary Nicotra households — and the search stands here.
Brisbane
Queensland · 103 events · from 1925

Where the four lines met. Thirteen of them lie at Nudgee — the same cemetery as the Falco.

What this archive has established

A claim, tested

The Prostamo were said to reach 1697. They are documented to Domenico Prostamo, a sailor of Briatico dead before 1886 — and asserted, by a tree already shown to be six years out elsewhere, for four generations above him. The audit →

Four plots in a row

All four of Michael Rocco Mazza's grandparents — born in three towns six hundred kilometres apart, arrived on four different sailings — lie in consecutive plots V-1026 to V-1029 at Nudgee. The register →

Nine ships, named

The family tree recorded eighteen crossings and not one vessel. The National Archives passenger index named nine of them — the Toscana, the Surriento, the Roma, the Palermo. The crossings →

The hundred and ten, joined

The export arrived as fourteen disconnected pieces, the second holding 110 Piedimonte Etneo Mazza attached to this family by nothing. Five register acts proved them the descendants of Salvatore's brothers. The argument →

Fifty-eight names it refuses to merge

Four men called Rosario Mazza, four called Giuseppe Arena, none with a date between them. The archive keeps them apart rather than inventing a descent. The method →

A standing rule

Dates are withheld for anyone 80 or younger

Everyone in this tree is written up in full — name, places, family, photographs, records. But 131 people who would be 80 or younger today, and whose death is not recorded, have their dates withheld. Those dates are stripped in the build, not hidden by the page: they are not in the HTML and not recoverable from this site. A removal request is honoured within days, without argument and without requiring a reason. The method →

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
