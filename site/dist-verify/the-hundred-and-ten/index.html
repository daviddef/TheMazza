The Hundred and Ten

From the day this archive was built its largest open question was a hundred and ten Piedimonte Etneo Mazza joined to the family by nothing at all. They are Salvatore's brothers' descendants.

The export this archive was built from was fourteen disconnected pieces. The largest held the family. The second held a hundred and ten Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo — a complete kindred with its own marriages, children and cousins — headed by a Rosario Mazza born 1850, married to a Leonarda with no surname, and attached to Michael Mazza's line by nothing.

Separately, the family tree gave Salvatore Mazza a father called Rosario about whom one thing was asserted and nothing was known. Two Rosario Mazza in one small town. The archive refused to merge them for a day, on the grounds that a shared name is not evidence.

Five records now say they are one man.

The argument

Rosario Mazza and Leonarda Bonanno, and their sons

An Italian marriage proclamation states the ages of both the bride and groom and their parents. Three of them, across twelve years, name the same couple and give Rosario's age each time — 53 in 1898, 55 in 1902, 65 in 1910. Two birth acts do the same — 28 in 1873, 31 in 1877, 32 in 1880. One man, born about 1845, married to Leonarda Bonanno, of Piedimonte Etneo.

The tree's Rosario has three sons — Orazio, Ignazio and Giovanni — by a wife called Leonarda. The register gives Rosario and Leonarda Bonanno six sons, and three of them are Orazio, Giovanni and Ignazio. The fourth is Salvatore Mazza , who sailed nothing and stayed put, and whose son Sebastiano Mazza took the Toscana to Australia in 1949.

And the name that seals it

Mariano

Salvatore's act of birth calls his father «Rosario Mazza di Mariano» — son of Mariano. Nothing in the family tree names a Mariano in that generation.

But the tree's unjoined hundred and ten contains a Mariano Mazza, and he is a son of Orazio — Rosario's son. Under the Sicilian naming custom a man's first son takes his own father's father's name, so Orazio naming a boy Mariano is exactly what you would expect if Orazio's grandfather was Mariano.

The custom and the document agree — and this time the document was read twice. Earlier today this archive built the same kind of argument on the name Venerio and had to withdraw it, because the reading underneath it was wrong. «Di Mariano» is written plainly, in a clerk's careful hand, and the Mariano in the tree was entered by a relative who had never seen the act.

How the archive made the join

A declared argument, not an automatic rule

This build merges two records automatically only on evidence found inside the export — a shared birth year, or the same family standing around them. That rule was written after an earlier version fused four undated Rosario Mazza on the strength of the name alone and manufactured a pedigree. It would never have made this join, and it was right not to.

So this join is declared, in data/documented-joins.tsv, with its citation attached, and applied after the automatic rules as an explicit override. The difference matters: an automatic merge is a rule; a documented join is an argument, and an argument has to be published so it can be attacked.

The export holds this man twice: as an undated stub who is Salvatore Mazza's father (@I22@, component 1), and as "Rosario Mazza b. 1850 of Piedimonte Etneo" married to a Leonarda with no surname, heading the 110-person component 2 (@I23@). FIVE independent records make them one man. (1) Nati 1873 atto 3: Salvatore Mazza born 5 Jan 1873 to ROSARIO MAZZA DI MARIANO, 28, and LEONARDA BONANNO. (2) Marriage proclamation 1898: Mazza Salvatore, 25, son of Rosario, 53, and Bonanno Leonarda. (3) Marriage proclamation 1902: Mazza GIOVANNI, 27, son of Rosario, 55, and Bonanno Leonarda. (4) Marriage proclamation 1910: Mazza IGNAZIO, 23, son of Rosario, 65, and Bonanno Leonarda, both parents present and consenting. (5) Nati 1877 atto 168: ORAZIO Mazza born to Rosario, 31, and Leonarda. The tree's @I23@ has exactly these sons - Orazio, Ignazio, Giovanni - by a wife called Leonarda. Rosario's ages give a birth about 1845; the tree says 1850.

What would overturn it: Rosario Mazza's own act of marriage, or his death act, naming a father who is not Mariano — or a second Rosario Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, born about 1845, married to a second Leonarda Bonanno, with sons of the same four names. The archive would rather that possibility were tested than assumed away. The queue →

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
