Catania, Sicily

Giarre

A town named by a document and contradicted by its own registers. The largest comune in this archive, and the one that holds the clearest negative in it.

21 September 2026 · «nato a Giarre»

Six words on a printed form

Piedimonte's Matrimoni allegati of 1872, fascicolo 20, holds the publication certificate for Giovanni Nicotra and Venera Cassaniti. The 1872 form prints «nato a ____» separately from «residente a ____», and the clerk filled both:

TRA GIOVANNI NICOTRA — nato a GIARRE — residente a MASCALI — di professione bracciante — di età anni trentuno

That sentence is why this comune is in the archive at all. It is also, so far, the only evidence that anybody in this family was ever here.

Eleven years read end to end, and he is in none of them

Giarre's annual Tavola is alphabetical and names both parents, so a Nicotra of a Rosario would identify itself without an act being opened.

The only Nicotra household at Giarre in eleven years is a titled one. Dottor Don Alfio Nicotra and Donna Antonina Consoli name a Salvatore in November 1846 and a Salvatore again in January 1848 — which is what a family does when the first child dies. Giovanni's father was a bracciante. Different household, different condition.

22 September 2026 · found in another town's register

There were untitled Nicotra at Giarre after all

The eleven years above are still eleven years, and they still do not hold Giovanni. But the sentence they seemed to support — that Giarre's Nicotra were all gentry — was never what they said. It was what eleven years of one register could not see.

Riposto's marriage Tavola for 1844, atto 39, marries Nicotra Salvadore to Maria Barbagallo. The patria column gives his as GIARRE. His parents are written plainly, with no Don and no Donna: Alfio Nicotra and Rosa Pappalardo. He then registers two children at Riposto, in 1845 and 1847.

That is an ordinary Giarre Nicotra family, documented — and invisible in Giarre's own birth indexes. Salvadore married in 1844, so he was born about 1815 to 1824, which is before the earliest year this archive has read here. It does not find Giovanni. It does something better for the argument: it shows that a Nicotra can be nato a Giarre, be entirely absent from the Giarre years that have been read, and turn up in Riposto's registers instead — which is exactly what Giovanni's certificate and Giarre's silence together require of him.

a caution this comune taught

The alphabet starts again after Z

The 1848 index says so itself, in the clerk's own hand, in the Osservazioni column beside the Nicotra entry: «in fine dell'alfabeto riportata altra volta la lettera M» — at the end of the alphabet the letter M is entered a second time.

Giarre's Tavole append extra letter-sections after Z for acts registered late. Nine of the eleven years do it; 1846 and 1847 do not, and end at Z with a blank leaf. So a volume with no appendix looks exactly like a volume whose appendix you have not reached, and «the alphabet finished» is not the same as «the index finished». Giarre holds two copies of every year. Each register appears twice under different segnature — 63 Nati volumes for roughly half that many years, and the same doubling in 57 Morti and 46 Matrimoni. A leaf lost in one copy may survive in the other, which is a resource this archive has nowhere else, and has not yet needed to use. Unlike Mascali it does have 1844.

Three readings, and none of them proved

Giovanni Nicotra's certificate says Giarre. Giarre's registers, read for eleven consecutive years in both the main run and the appendix, do not hold him. Something has to give, and this archive does not yet know what.

  • His ages are wrong again. He is trentuno in October 1872 and ventotto in December 1875 — already six years apart in three. The windows either side of the read decade are 1833–37 and 1849–53, and neither has been touched.
  • He was registered at Riposto, the lower town, which has registered separately since 1821. This reading now has a case of its own behind it: Nicotra Salvadore, patria Giarre, married at Riposto in 1844 and registered his children there. It is still not Giovanni, and one man's habit is not a rule — but it is no longer an inference from general history alone.
  • «Giarre» is loose for a frazione — Macchia, Trepunti, Altarello, San Giovanni Montebello.

Nothing above is a finding. It is what is left when a document and a register disagree and both have been read. The Nicotra line → · The open questions →

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
