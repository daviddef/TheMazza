The register the others do not keep

89 open, 181 in all

Every other register on this site answers what we know. What Has Been Read says what has been searched, negatives included; Open Questions says what the records have not answered; Corrections says where a document and the family tree disagree. None of them says what is actually being worked on, who is waiting for whom, or what was deliberately abandoned and why — so that lived in a chat window, which is the one place that does not survive the session. This is that page. It is kept in the same shape as the other seven archives in this estate, and a build check refuses if it slips.

Messina before 1820 — abandoned, and why

A deliberate stop, not a failure. Michele Donato was born at Messina about 1814 and his parents Candeloro Donato and Maria Paladino married earlier still. Probing the whole province year by year — 1815, 1819, 1820, 1821, 1825, 1840 — returns nothing before 1820 and 366 volumes in 1820. Sicily had no Napoleonic period, so there is no earlier series to fall back on. Anything before 1820 is a parish record held by the Archdiocese of Messina and is not on Antenati. So Candeloro Donato is where the Scilla line stops, exactly as Pasquale Prostamo is where the Calabrian one does. A second wall may sit behind it: the comune of Messina appeared in no year probed although its series claims 1069 volumes, which is plausibly the earthquake of 28 December 1908 — recorded as a thing to check, not as a finding.

The nine unnamed crossings, starting with Domenica Prostamo in March 1951

Nine of eighteen crossings are documented. Domenica Prostamo, March 1951, is in the index under neither Prostamo nor Mazza. Also open: Michael Antonio Polistena, 28 May 1950, and Francesca Prostamo, who sails on the Roma with Francessco on 10 October 1957 and is in no tree at all. RE-OPENED 16 SEPTEMBER. Domenica Prostamo's March 1951 crossing was the one this row thought was nearly settled. It is not: the Ugolino Vivaldi identification has been read off the manifest and disproved (row 110). She is back to a month and a year with no ship. What the disproof also teaches is how to search now. The NAA index gives a name, a date and a ship; the manifests give a destination and a port of debarkation, and those are what actually distinguish one Italian family from another in 1951. A woman joining her husband in Brisbane should appear on a manifest marked for Sydney with a Queensland address — not Fremantle, whatever the item title says. That is a much sharper filter than a surname, and it can be applied to every 1951 arrival out of Genoa rather than only to the ones whose name nearly matches. 20 SEPTEMBER: ONE OF THE THREE IS LOCATED, AND DOMENICA IS NOT. Francesca Prostamo has her own record — NAA: BP25/1, *«Trungadi [nee Prostamo], Francesca — arrived Brisbane on the T/v Roma 21 October 1957»*. She is the daughter the manifest already showed, travelling with her father; what is new is that the card carries a married surname, which is a name to search on rather than a second woman. And it corrects her father's arrival and hers. This archive had Francesco Prostamo at Fremantle on 10 October, graded *documented*. The Brisbane aliens series says Brisbane, 21 October — the ship's landfall against the man's debarkation, eleven days apart. Both true; the second is the one that explains a Queensland family. Domenica Prostamo is still nowhere. POSTAMO, the spelling Queensland's registrar uses on her daughter's death registration, returns zero from NameSearch — control-tested against PROSTAMO, which returns thirteen in the same minute. She exists in the National Archives only inside her husband's file title, *J25 1971/1074, «Mazza, Sebastiano; wife Domenica [nee Prostamo]»*. The destination-and-port method this row describes is still the way in.

From row 32 they run as a roadmap: **32–34** finish the Ventrice hunt; **35–37** the cheap sweeps the annual-Tavola trick unlocked; **38–47** the spine frontier town by town, which is where the parentless ancestors actually are; **48–50** Australia; **51–55** the structural work that decides whether the site can show what the archive knows; **56–57** what only a living relative can answer; **58–63** what the September walls left behind. **64–88 are the second tranche, raised after those walls were established:** **64–72** the Italian records the walls did *not* close — processetti, death registers, and two towns never queried; **73–77** structure; **78–80** Australia; **81–83** the estate and the method; **84–88** the loose ends today's work exposed. Items founded on a mistake are struck through rather than deleted, the same way a retracted claim is.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski

  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
