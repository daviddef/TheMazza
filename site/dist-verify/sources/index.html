Read this page with the method beside it. An index entry is a real source and a weak one: it proves a name was written somewhere, and it does not show you the handwriting, the parents, the ages or the address. Every row below says which it is. Of the 39 sources named in the family's own export, only 11 individuals carry a citation at all — which is why so much of this archive is now resting on registers the export never mentioned.

12 of the 25 link to the thing itself. The rest are the indexes the tree cites and this archive has never opened — left unlinked on purpose, because a link would say they had been reached.

The family tree

Mazza Family Tree, MyHeritage the tree

www.myheritage.com

Exported 9 September 2026 from the Defranceski family site. 614 individuals, 212 families, 133 photographs. Contributed by Michael Mazza and relatives.

Used for: The spine of this archive, and the thing every other source is being used to test.

Italian civil registers, read in the original

Antenati — Scilla, Reggio Calabria read here

antenati.cultura.gov.it/search-registry/?localita=Scilla

Stato civile napoleonico 1809-1815, restaurazione 1816-1865, italiano 1866-1943. 327 volumes listed here.

Used for: The Arena spine from Rocco back to FILIPPO ARENA and SANTA UNGARO in the 1720s; the Arlotta, the Costa, the Donato and the Polistena.

Antenati — Scilla, Matrimoni processetti read here

antenati.cultura.gov.it/search-registry/?localita=Scilla

Twenty-two volumes, 1810-1865. Each bundle holds the BIRTH EXTRACTS of both spouses and the DEATH ACTS of dead parents, copied out of the parish books.

Used for: Filippo Arena's baptism of 25 March 1793, Domenica Costa's of 16 June 1787, and Giovanni de Costa's death of 29 March 1790 -- the oldest dated event in this archive.

The processetti sit in the same Scilla holding as the registers above; the portal filters them by tipologia once the place is open.

Antenati — Piedimonte Etneo, Catania read here

antenati.cultura.gov.it/search-registry/?localita=Piedimonte%20Etneo

457 registers, 1820-1893, plus nine volumes of Riveli di anime e di terre from 1714 and 1747-1752.

Used for: The Mazza spine to MARIANO MAZZA and MARIA CATALANO, born about 1809; four more children for Rosario and Leonarda; and the absence of any Mazza in the town's 1747 household census.

Antenati — Briatico, Zambrone, Pizzo, Potenzoni read here

antenati.cultura.gov.it/search-registry/?localita=Briatico

The Calabrian side. Briatico has 555 registers of which this archive has listed 80.

Used for: The Prostamo to 1697, the Ventrice, the Cichello and the Melluso -- and the 1849 marriage bundle that proved three generations at once.

The link opens Briatico, the first of the four. Zambrone, Pizzo and Potenzoni are separate localita searches on the same portal.

Antenati — the name index read here

antenati.cultura.gov.it/search-nominative

Some comuni have their acts indexed BY NAME with parents and spouse: Scilla deaths 1911-1943, Pizzo births 1868-1903, Catania deaths 1911 onward.

Used for: One query replaces a volume sweep where it exists. Five of the eight comuni this family touches are not indexed at all.

Australian records, read in the original

National Archives of Australia — RecordSearch read here

recordsearch.naa.gov.au

Internee dossiers, aliens registration, naturalisation, immigration correspondence. The item TITLE carries the particulars even when nothing is digitised.

Used for: Three men interned in the Second World War; Maria Nicotra's arrival at Sydney on 20 April 1962; the ships behind five family crossings.

Queensland Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages — historical index index only

www.familyhistory.bdm.qld.gov.au

Births 1829-1926, marriages 1829-1951, deaths 1829-1996. The index carries the MOTHER and PARENT columns.

Used for: Twenty-eight registrations, including the one that names John Nicotra and Venera Cassaneti -- and the sweep that showed «Cassaneti» appears exactly once in the whole index.

Brisbane Catholic Cemeteries — Nudgee index only

brisbane.discovereverafter.com

The interment register, searchable by name, with plot numbers.

Used for: Thirteen burials, several of them adjacent, and the married names that identified four women the tree had lost.

Tried and found wanting

FindMyPast index only

www.findmypast.com.au

A six-day trial, swept systematically rather than browsed.

Used for: Its Italian births, marriages and deaths sets DO NOT COVER SCILLA -- zero Polistena in all three. Recorded in full, negatives included, so that nobody pays to discover it again.

Trove a wall

trove.nla.gov.au/search/category/newspapers

The National Library of Australia's newspaper archive.

Used for: A WALL, not a nil: its own vendor bundle throws on a storage API this browser lacks. A free NLA API key would settle it.

The National Archives, Kew -- Discovery API a wall

discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk

Free JSON, no key.

Used for: Swept for this family and it is not there. The empty-202 throttle reads as zero results, which is how a wall gets mistaken for a negative.

What is not here yet

This section used to begin “No Italian civil register has been read for this family. That is the largest single gap.” It was true on the day the archive was built and it is kept here, struck, because an archive that quietly deletes its own old headline is harder to trust than one that shows the correction.

What is actually missing now is narrower and harder.

  • The parish books of Scilla. Everything above the 1720s on the Arena side is pre-civil. The 1815 marriage bundle names the books and the men who kept them — the liber Baptizatorum and liber Defunctorum of the Chiesa Matrice, under archpriest D. Giuseppe Ingigneri. The Archdiocese of Reggio Calabria–Bova is the holder, and nothing of it is online.
  • Piedimonte Etneo after 1893. Antenati stops there. The marriage of about 1900 and a death in 1925 sit in the Tribunale duplicate for Catania, which runs to 1929.
  • Four files at Brisbane and one alien registration file that would state where Maria Nicotra was born. Open access, not digitised, and a copy request rather than a search.
  • Zambrone's deaths, 1813–1821. A real hole in the state's copy; the comune's own second copy is the only route, and that is a letter.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.

An evidence-first archive of the Mazza family of Piedimonte Etneo in Sicily — and of Briatico and Scilla in Calabria, of New York and Buenos Aires, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence, or is marked as unproven. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
