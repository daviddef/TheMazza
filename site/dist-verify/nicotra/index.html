The Nicotra

Three days ago this line was two names on a Queensland death certificate. It now reaches generation six on both sides, crosses four comuni — and the man it is named for is still missing.

Where it started

Everything this archive knew about the Nicotra came from one Australian document: the 1963 Queensland death registration of Maria Nicotra , which names her parents as «John Nicotra» and «Venera Cassaneti». No dates, no places, no act — and one of those surnames does not exist in Italy. «Cassaneti» appears exactly once in the whole Queensland index, on that certificate, taken down from an informant at a Brisbane registry counter. The Sicilian form is CASSANITI, and a register searched for the other spelling returns nothing forever.

The line, as four acts give it

Read in the original, September 2026. Not one of these people is in the family's export with a date.

generation 6

Rosario Nicotra × Venera Murabito

Of MASCALI, both living and working there in October 1872 — he a bracciante, she a contadina. Named without «fu» in an act that writes «fu» for a witness two lines above, so the absence is a statement. Neither has been found in any register of any of the four towns.

Piedimonte Etneo, Matrimoni 1872 atto 20

generation 6

Gaetano Cassaniti × Maria Currenti

Of PIEDIMONTE ETNEO, and BOTH DEAD by October 1872 — «figlia del fu GAETANO, e di fu MARIA CURRENTI». Gaetano is also the father named in his daughter's birth entry of 1848, which is what ties the birth to the marriage.

Piedimonte Etneo, Matrimoni 1872 atto 20; Nati 1848 atto 45

generation 5

Giovanni Nicotra × Venera Cassaniti

Married at Piedimonte Etneo on 7 OCTOBER 1872 at nine in the morning. He thirty-one, a bracciante, «domiciliato e residente a MASCALI» and «nato a GIARRE»; she twenty-four, born at Piedimonte in 1848. The family's export calls them «John Nicotra» and «Venera Cassaneti» and gives neither a date.

Piedimonte Etneo, Matrimoni 1872 atto 20 and Matrimoni allegati fascicolo 20

generation 4

Maria Nicotra

Born at MASCALI on 8 DECEMBER 1875 at half past nine in the evening, in contrada Sopra Pietrafitta, and registered two days later by her father in person. Married Salvatore Mazza about 1900; widowed 1929; sailed to Sydney on 20 April 1962 at about eighty-six, to her sons; died at Brisbane in 1963 and lies at Nudgee.

Mascali, Nati 1875 atto 144

The join is not circular, which matters because «Giovanni Nicotra» is a common name. The family's own export — parsed long before any of this — gives Giovanni and Venera seven children, and one of them is a ROSARIO. A first son takes the paternal grandfather's name, and the paternal grandfather in the 1872 act is Rosario. This archive held the grandson's name for months and could not have derived the grandfather's from it.

What 42 read years have not found

Rosario Nicotra and Venera Murabito are named in one act and appear in no register.

The instrument is not the problem. Every one of these Tavole is alphabetical and names both parents, so the household would announce itself. What is being tested is whether the man's own statements about himself are true — he is trentuno in 1872 and ventotto in 1875, six years apart in three — and whether «nato a Giarre» means the comune or the district.

The wrong Nicotra, and the right shape

Twelve years of Riposto's marriage Tavole were read for exactly the row that would end this: Rosario Nicotra and Venera Murabito, who would hand over generation seven in a single line. That row is not there. What is there is one N entry across the twelve years, and it is a different man.

He is the Salvadore Nicotra whose children the Riposto birth sweep had already turned up — Alfio in May 1845, Rosa in November 1847 — without knowing where he came from. Now it is written: born at Giarre, married at Riposto, children registered at Riposto.

This is not Giovanni's father and this archive does not pretend it is. What it settles is the question behind the search. «Nato a Giarre» and «absent from Giarre's registers» stopped being a contradiction the moment a second man did both. Whether Rosario is Salvadore's brother is the obvious next thought and has no evidence at all behind it — two men of a surname in one district are not kin until an act says so, and this archive has been wrong about exactly that before.

What would carry it further

Salvadore Nicotra's own birth act at Giarre. He married in 1844, so he was born about 1815–1824 — a decade earlier than anything read here. That act names Alfio Nicotra's age and trade, and would say whether this Giarre household is a labouring one or the Dottor Don line under another spelling.

And the marriage Tavole of Giarre and Mascali, neither of which has been opened. If Rosario married in his own town rather than his wife's, he is in one of them.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
