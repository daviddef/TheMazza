The Mazza Name Line

On the morning this archive was built the Mazza name reached back four generations and stopped at a man with no dates. It now runs seven, and every step in Sicily is a document.

The archive opened with an embarrassment: the family took its name from its shallowest line. The Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo could be followed back four generations to a Rosario Mazza about whom exactly one thing was asserted — that he fathered Salvatore — while the Calabrian Prostamo ran nine generations to 1697.

That is no longer true. On 10 September 2026 the Sicilian registers gave up Salvatore's act of birth, and with it his father's age, his father's street, and his father's father.

The act that did it

Piedimonte Etneo, Nati 1873, atto 3

…è comparso Rosario Mazza di Mariano, di anni ventotto, bracciante, qui domiciliato, il quale mi ha presentato un bambino di sesso maschile che dichiara essergli nato il giorno cinque corrente Gennaro alle ore sette antemeridiane, dalla di lui moglie Leonarda Bonanno di Gaetano, seco lui domiciliata nella casa di sua abitazione sita in questo Comune Contrada Terremorte, al quale figlio dichiara di dare il nome di Salvadore.

Three things in one sentence. Salvatore Mazza was born on 5 January 1873 — not 1879, as the family tree has always said. His father Rosario was twenty-eight, which puts Rosario's own birth about 1844. And Rosario is «di Mariano» — son of a Mariano Mazza whom nothing in this archive had ever named.

The line, generation by generation

Generation 1
Mariano Mazza
c. 1809

Aged THIRTY-SIX when he declared his son Rosario's birth on 2 June 1845, so born about 1809. A BRACCIALE then; 'fu Mariano gia contadino' by his son's death act of 1924. Named in the birth act of 1845, in the marriage act of 1871, in his grandson Salvatore's birth act of 1873 and in Rosario's death act of 1924 -- four records.

Married Maria Catalano. Aged THIRTY-SIX at her son Rosario's birth in June 1845, so born about 1809. 'fu Catalano Maria gia casalinga' in 1924. Named in the birth act of 1845, the marriage act of 1871 and the death act of 1924.

Source: Piedimonte Etneo, Nati 1845 atto 64; Matrimoni 1871 atto 14; Nati 1873 atto 3; Morti 1924

Generation 2
Rosario Mazza
2 June 1845 – 15 November 1924

BORN TO A DAY. Act 64 of the Piedimonte births of 1845: born at eleven in the morning of 2 JUNE 1845, in the house his parents lived in, and declared by his father Mariano the same day. Aged 28 in January 1873, 31 in 1877, 32 in 1880, 53 in 1898, 55 in 1902, 65 in 1910 and 79 at death -- eight records, one man. A bracciante, later contadino, of Contrada Terremorte, via Grotta Nicodemo and Contrada S. Basilio.

Married Leonarda Bonanno. Daughter of GAETANO BONANNO and ANNA CAVALLARO of Piedimonte Etneo, named together in the couple's marriage act. A BRACCIANTE in her own right in 1871. Married Rosario on 30 APRIL 1871, act 14. Still living in 1910, when she and Rosario consented in person to their son Ignazio's marriage.

Source: Nati 1845 atto 64; Matrimoni 1871 atto 14; Nati 1873 atto 3; Nati 1877 atto 168; Nati 1880 atto 66; proclamations 1898, 1902, 1910; Morti 1924

How the tree was wrong, and why it mattered

Six years, and eight wasted registers

The family tree gives Salvatore Mazza as born 1879 at Piedimonte Etneo, died 1929 at Catania. Acting on that, this archive read the birth registers of Piedimonte Etneo for 1875, 1876, 1877, 1878, 1879, 1880, 1881 and 1882, and those of Mascali and Giarre for 1879, and found no Salvatore Mazza in any of them.

He was born in 1873, six years earlier, and he died at Piedimonte Etneo, not Catania — in the frazione of Vena, at Contrada Grotta Nicodemo, on 19 September 1929, aged fifty-six.

The lesson is about search strategy, not about the tree. Eight registers were read on the strength of one undocumented year. What actually broke it open was working from a different record — the Queensland death index, which gave Salvatore's son Rosario a birth year of 1899 and his wife her full name — and then finding the parish marriage of 1898 and the civil death act of 1929, each of which names Salvatore's parents. Three records converged on the man before the register was opened at the right page.

And what it does to the hundred and ten

Mariano

The second component of the family tree — the hundred and ten Piedimonte Etneo Mazza joined to this family by nothing — is headed by a Rosario Mazza born 1850, and it contains a Mariano Mazza.

The register now gives a Rosario Mazza born about 1844 whose father was Mariano. That is a second, independent correspondence on top of the Orazio one — and it is the strongest reason yet to think the two halves are one family. It is still not the document that joins them. The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
