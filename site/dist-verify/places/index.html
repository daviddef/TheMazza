Four names, normalised

Why one town was three places

Scilla appeared in the export three times — as Scillla with three l's, as Scilla bare, and as Scilla, Reggio Calabria — so the map drew one dot and left two empty places beside it. Soriano Calabro was spelt Sorianno. Both are keystrokes, not claims about the family, so they are normalised when the places are built.

Nothing is edited silently. Every substitution is listed here, and the export itself is untouched.

As the export writes itAs the map needs itWhy
Scillla Scilla Three l's. A slip of the keyboard in the export; the town is Scilla, Reggio Calabria. Left alone it made a second, empty Scilla that the atlas could not place.
Sorianno Calabro Soriano Calabro Two n's. The comune is Soriano Calabro, Vibo Valentia — the Briatico end of the family.
Scilla Scilla, Reggio Calabria A third spelling of the same town, with the province left off. Merged so that one place is one place.
Soriano Calabro Soriano Calabro, Vibo Valentia, Calabria The province added so the gazetteer can find it. There is a Soriano nel Cimino in Lazio and a Soriano in Colombia; without the province and region the map has to guess — and every other Vibo Valentia place in this archive is written «Town, Vibo Valentia, Calabria».

And one coordinate set by hand. Soriano Calabro is not in the estate gazetteer, and asking for “Soriano Calabro, Italy” returns Soriano nel Cimino in Lazio — four hundred kilometres north, a different town with a similar name. The comune in Vibo Valentia is at 38.5969, 16.2304, and that coordinate is recorded in data/gazetteer-local.json with its source, because a dot in the wrong province is worse than no dot.

Every place with a page of its own

One page per place the atlas can put a person in, listing who was born, married or buried there and what the archive read to know it. These were reachable only by clicking a marker on the map, which meant a reader without JavaScript — and a search engine — could not get to any of them.

The other archives

The Mazza Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The Lerena Archive Rosario · the Cape · the Rand A horse-dealer out of Argentina, a stable under Table Mountain, and four family traditions tested against the record rather than repeated. Lerena · Taylor
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
