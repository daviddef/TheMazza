# The sections of an index are not always in alphabetical order

Written 23 September 2026 by the Mazza session, for the estate, in the same
spirit as `plates-held-and-never-shown.md` was written for me. **This is data,
not a task.** Nothing outside this archive has been changed, and the shared kit
has deliberately not been touched.

It is here rather than in the kit's `traps.md` because this archive's standing
instruction is to leave the kit alone. If it belongs in `traps.md`, somebody
with that remit should move it.

## The trap

Clearing a year from an annual *Tavola* has an obvious shortcut: find the
section for the letter you want, and check it into the next letter. If **M**
runs straight into **P**, there is no **N**, and the year is a negative in one
look. Two comuni in this archive index that way and it works.

**At Mascali it does not.** The marriage *Tavola* of **1847** writes its
section headings in the order **C, then N, then M**. 1841 and 1835 put **V
before S**. It is not one clerk slipping — it is how that office worked, across
years and hands.

The plate is on `/mascali/`, because this is much more convincing seen than
described.

## What it cost

Nine Mascali birth years, 1839–1848, had been cleared at the junction before
this was known. They are now marked **provisional** on the page and on
work-list row 180. Nothing *found* was withdrawn — a household discovered in
1840 and 1847 is still there. Only the **negatives** got weaker, and a negative
is most of what a sweep produces.

## The rule

**At a new comune, read one whole Tavola first** — first heading to «Fine» —
before trusting any junction anywhere in that series. `tools/montage_pages.py`
in this repo makes that affordable: it crops the same narrow strip from several
pages and stands them side by side, so a volume's whole table is one look. It
locates; it does not read. A surname taken off a narrow band has been wrong
twice here, so the entry itself is always re-read at full width.

## Why it is the same trap as two others

The estate's `traps.md` opens with «a trap described in the abstract is one
nobody recognises when they are in it». This one has now appeared three times
in this archive wearing different clothes, and the family resemblance is
**«the section's end is not where you assume»**:

- **The alphabet is repeated after Z.** Giarre's clerks append extra
  letter-sections past the end for late-registered acts, and say so in the
  *Osservazioni* column. Reaching Z is not reaching the end of the index.
- **A section runs across a page break**, and a reading that stopped at the
  foot of a page missed a name four entries later.
- **The sections are not in order at all**, which is this note.

The first two are about where a section *ends*. The third is about where it
*begins*, and it breaks the same shortcut.
